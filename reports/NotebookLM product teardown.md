# Gemini Notebook Earns Trust, Leaves Multimodal Gaps

> **Update (2026-11-06): hands-on testing corrected parts of this desk research.** See [docs/research/notebooklm-hands-on-results.md](../docs/research/notebooklm-hands-on-results.md).
> - **Scans, figures and tables:** all handled well in testing, contrary to the reports below.
> - **Citations:** on scans they land at the top of the source, and on YouTube they highlight the whole transcript.
> - **Video:** there are no timestamps, and `.vtt` and `.srt` transcript files are rejected.
> - **Speed and accuracy:** answers average about 20 s to the first word, with 95.8% accuracy and 12.5% of responses containing an unsupported claim.
> - **Usage:** an estimate bar appears before each generation.
> - **Organisation:** Collections now exist.
>
> Where this report and the hands-on results disagree, trust the hands-on results.

As of November 2026, Gemini Notebook (Google renamed NotebookLM on **July 16, 2026**) is a source-grounded study workspace. Users load **50 to roughly 600 sources per notebook**, each capped at **500,000 words or 200 MB**. They chat with those sources and every answer carries inline passage citations. A Studio panel turns the same corpus into about ten one-click outputs: two-host audio "podcasts", explainer, short and cinematic videos, slide decks, infographics, mind maps, reports, flashcards, quizzes and data tables. Fast Research and Deep Research find web sources to add, and since July each notebook has a code-running "cloud computer". Since **September 2, 2026**, usage runs on an undisclosed compute budget that refreshes every five hours; paid plans multiply it (Plus 2x, Pro 4x, Ultra more). Answer quality is good but thinly evidenced. In small published studies it put an unsupported claim in **13% of responses, against about 40%** for general chatbots, and found the right supporting passage **95% of the time**. It still gets numerical comparisons wrong, adds interpretation the sources don't support, gives inconsistent rubric judgements and runs long. No study has tested the current Gemini 3.5-era product. Google has never published the architecture. Staff statements and observed behaviour point to a **hybrid of long-context stuffing and retrieval over text extracted from every source**, with Studio outputs written as LLM-planned structured outputs and then rendered by Google's speech, image and video models. That text-first core is the main weakness. YouTube import is caption-only. Scanned pages, tables, math and figures degrade. Citations land in extracted text rather than on the original page. For Learnin, the Claude + Qdrant + Neon + Cloudflare app this report serves, the lesson has three parts. Copy NotebookLM's inputs-conversation-outputs model, its hover-to-verify citation loop and its handoff from studio outputs back into chat. Beat it on multimodal ingestion and precise locators, which Learnin's design already plans. Depart from it on transparency: show when the app is retrieving rather than reading whole sources, label outside knowledge, and publish budgets and evals. One caveat covers the whole report: the research notes rest on search-result summaries rather than full-page reads, and many sources are third-party guides, so figures labelled as reported are weaker evidence than Google's own statements.

## Ten Studio outputs and a five-hour compute budget define the 2026 product

The rename was cosmetic in substance but strategic in direction. Google calls Gemini Notebook the same standalone product, now "updated with a secure cloud computer" and reachable across more of Google's ecosystem ([blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-notebook/notebooklm-gemini-notebook/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/16/google-continues-its-renaming-streak-by-turning-notebooklm-to-gemini-notebook/)). Notebooks appeared inside the Gemini mobile app on **April 30, 2026** ([pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/1697/notebooklm-april-2026-mobile-cinematic-video-gemini-sync)). A third-party guide reports that the Gemini app could create and edit notebooks from **October 23, 2026** ([Glasp](https://glasp.co/articles/notebooklm-2026)). One outlet puts the user base moved to the new brand at about **30 million** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-kills-notebooklm-name-moving-30-million-users-to-gemini-notebook/)). The notebook is becoming a grounded context container that the rest of Google's AI surfaces can use. It is no longer only a destination app.

**Sources.** Ingestion covers PDFs, text and Markdown, pasted text, web URLs, YouTube, audio files, Google Docs, Slides and Sheets, Word files, images, and **EPUB**, which Google added for all users in March 2026 ([Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/03/new-ways-to-customize-and-interact-with-your-content-in-NotebookLM.html); [blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/notebooklm-deep-research-file-types/)). The official per-source cap is **500,000 words or 200 MB** on every plan ([Gemini Notebook Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187?hl=en)). The enterprise documentation adds that a single sheet can hold about **150,000 active cells** ([Google Cloud docs](https://cloud.google.com/agentspace/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/overview)).

Every format is reduced to text. Audio is transcribed and queried as a transcript. YouTube videos import only if they are public and captioned, and only the transcript comes in. Websites lose images, embedded media and paywalled content ([Gemini Notebook Help mirror](https://webproxy.poorya-velaei-d67.workers.dev/https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16215270); [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/notebooklm-upload-failed/)). Google Drive sources are snapshots that the user must re-sync by hand ([sourclip](https://www.sourclip.com/blog/notebooklm-troubleshooting)).

**Chat.** Chat is grounded Q&A over the sources the user has checked. A notebook-level Configure dialog offers three goals: **Default**, **Learning Guide** (a tutor that asks follow-up questions instead of only answering) and **Custom** (up to 10,000 characters of instructions). Length can be Default, Longer or Shorter. The settings apply to chat and to every Studio output ([UChicago Academic Tech](https://academictech.uchicago.edu/2026/04/06/google-notebooklm-an-ai-tool-for-research-and-studying/); [MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/notebooklm-settings-configure-notebook/)).

The October 29, 2025 engine upgrade brought an **8x larger context window** (about 1M tokens), **6x longer conversation memory**, saved history and a self-reported **50% gain in satisfaction** on answers that draw on many sources ([blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/notebooklm-custom-personas-engine-upgrade/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/gemini/notebooklm-just-got-a-major-chat-upgrade-with-8x-more-context-better-memory-and-smarter-responses)). History reached all users on **December 17, 2025**. It syncs across devices and stays private to each user in a shared notebook ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/notebooklm-chat-history-rollout-3625900); [fonearena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/?p=467636)). Gemini 3.5 became the default model in June 2026. In the same update, chat started suggesting and adding web sources ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/notebooklms-new-update-will-help-you-build-source-repository-from-chat/)).

**Studio.** Studio is where most of the feature growth has gone.

Discovery lives in the source panel. **Fast Research** returns about ten annotated web sources in seconds. **Deep Research** "can automatically browse up to hundreds of websites" in the background and writes a multi-page report. Before anything is imported, the user reviews both the cited and the uncited sources it found ([Gemini Notebook Help](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16215270?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)).

**Sharing.** Notebooks can be shared publicly by link. Curated "featured notebooks" from publishers such as The Economist and The Atlantic launched in July 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/notebooklm-adds-featured-notebooks-from-the-economist-the-atlantic-and-others/)). People viewing a featured or public notebook can read its sources, chat with it and see existing artifacts, but cannot generate new ones ([Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16322204?hl=en)).

**Code execution.** Code execution on the new cloud computer reached Ultra and Workspace AI Ultra accounts first. Pro is reportedly due "later" ([Geeky Gadgets](https://www.geeky-gadgets.com/notebooklm-2026-new-features/)).

## A three-panel layout and a hover-to-verify loop carry the UX

Since the **December 2024 redesign**, a notebook has had three columns: **Sources** on the left, **Chat** in the centre and **Studio** on the right ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/?p=653973); [blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/notebooklm-new-features-december-2024/)). University guides written after the 2026 rename still describe the same three columns ([UIC](https://learning.uic.edu/resources/google-ai-for-education-accelerator-program/ai-tools-and-resources-gemini-notebook/navigating-gemini-notebook/)). The layout has held for two years while the product added video, slides, infographics, quizzes, research and code. New outputs became tiles in Studio, never new panels. Inputs on the left, conversation in the middle, outputs on the right is the mental model, and it scales.

The first screen of a notebook does real work. When it opens, chat shows a generated overview of all the sources ([UIC](https://learning.uic.edu/resources/google-ai-for-education-accelerator-program/ai-tools-and-resources-gemini-notebook/navigating-gemini-notebook/)). Clicking a source opens a **Source guide** card with a summary and clickable key-topic chips; each chip asks chat for a cited explainer of that topic ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1611774/google-notebooklm-generative-ai-notes-app.html)). Each source row has a checkbox, and only checked sources feed answers ([capturewhitespace](https://capturewhitespace.com/journal/gemini-notebook-setup-and-interface)). That makes scope a persistent, visible control rather than an @-mention. The drawback is hidden state: users forget what they have unchecked.

The trust loop is the product's signature. Answers carry small numbered citation markers. Hovering one shows the quoted passage, and clicking it scrolls the source viewer to the passage and highlights it ([Gemini Notebook Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/14276569?hl=en)). Since March 2025, a response saved as a note keeps its citations ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/3/notebooklm-introduces-new-citation-feature)).

The loop depends on a design choice that is easy to miss. The viewer shows **extracted text, not the original file**. Bulk export likewise returns parsed text, never the original PDFs ([sourclip export guide](https://www.sourclip.com/guides/notebooklm-export-guide)). Highlighting is trivially precise in that view, because a citation is a character range. The cost is that users lose page layout, figures and page numbers as reference points. For YouTube, citations link into the transcript ([blog.google](https://blog.google/technology/ai/notebooklm-audio-video-sources/)). One blogger found a cited timestamp that matched neither the content nor any timestamp in the transcript, so timestamp-precise citation is unconfirmed ([penchan.co](https://penchan.co/ai/notebooklm/)).

Studio outputs generate in the background while the user keeps working. Finished artifacts appear in the panel list. The notes found no push or email notification when a job completes ([supademo](https://supademo.com/blog/how-to-generate-an-audio-overview-in-notebooklm)). The strongest pedagogical pattern is the **handoff from an artifact back into chat**: clicking a mind-map node posts a question to chat, and users ask chat to explain a flashcard because the explanation comes back cited ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/cant-use-notebooklm-without-these-features/)). Interactive audio goes further. A listener taps "Join", the hosts call on them, answer from the sources and then resume. Reviewers report glitches such as the hosts swapping speakers ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/i-regret-ignoring-notebooklm-interactive-audio-overview/)).

Mobile does a narrower job than the web app. The iOS and Android apps arrived in May 2025. They focus on background and offline Audio Overview playback and on capture through the share sheet ([blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/notebooklm-app/)). Android Central found the app "mainly a player for Audio Overviews" ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/ai/the-notebooklm-app-for-android-kept-making-me-want-to-go-back-to-the-website)). A July 2026 review notes it forgets the playback position when closed mid-episode ([thebusinessdive](https://thebusinessdive.com/notebooklm-review)).

The most persistent complaint is organisation. Google said in February 2026 that folders were coming, with no timeline ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/notebooklm-folder-organization-3641948/)), and notebooks were still flat in July. Weak note-taking, vague import errors and source caps follow ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/please-stop-using-notebooklm-as-a-note-taking-app/); [vidnotes](https://vidnotes.app/blog/207-How-to-Fix-NotebookLM-Cant-Import-YouTube-Video-Comments-Disabled)). One Chrome extension advertises "35+ features" for organising and exporting, which shows how large the unmet need is ([nlmtools](https://www.nlmtools.com/features)).

## Small studies show 13% hallucination and 95% passage hits

Every published evaluation found NotebookLM more grounded than general chatbots on document tasks. Every one is small, and none tests the Gemini 3.x product. Several tested 2024–25 models, which the 2025 context upgrade and the 2026 model change may have outdated.

| Study | Setup | Headline result | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hagar, Agustianto & Diakopoulos, 2025 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25498v1)) | Newsroom queries over a document set; ChatGPT, Gemini, NotebookLM | Responses with ≥1 hallucination: **13%** for NotebookLM (2/15) vs ~40% for ChatGPT (6/15) and Gemini (4/10) | n=40 responses; directional only |
| Lung-cancer staging, 2024 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11604-024-01705-1)) | 100 fictional CT cases, Japanese staging guidance as source | **86%** accuracy vs 39% for GPT-4o with the same material and 25% without; **95%** correct reference location | Older model; numeric-threshold errors persisted |
| Pancreatic staging, 2025 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15664)) | NotebookLM on Gemini 2.0 Flash vs Gemini 2.0 Flash alone | Grounded version more accurate | Authors flag clinical data-security concerns |
| Risk-of-bias appraisal, 2025 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41503344/)) | Repeated runs on a pediatric orthopaedics rubric | Moderate-to-poor agreement between runs; "Not Ready for Prime Time" | Clearest negative result |
| Superconductivity experts ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03782); [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/testing-llms-on-superconductivity-research-questions/)) | 12 experts, 67 questions, 6 systems; NotebookLM loaded with 1,726 papers | Highest on evidence support; **least succinct** | Older model generation |
| Salinas, Nyarko et al., 2026 ([Stanford PDF](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/salinas_et_al.pdf)) | 16 contracts professors judged 2,918 human-vs-AI comparisons | NotebookLM beat professors **74.75%** of the time; plain Gemini 2.5 Pro 75.92% | Working paper; does not measure learning |

The error profile matters more than the headline rate. In the newsroom study most errors were not invented names or numbers. They were **"interpretive overconfidence"**, meaning characterisations the sources don't support, and **"attribution loss"**, where one person's quoted opinion is restated as fact ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25498v1)). The staging studies show the second weakness. NotebookLM retrieved the right guidance and still compared tumour sizes against thresholds incorrectly ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11604-024-01705-1)). The risk-of-bias paper shows the third: grounding did not make judgement against a rubric repeatable ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41503344/)).

The law study adds a subtler point. Grounded NotebookLM and ungrounded Gemini 2.5 Pro finished within about one percentage point of each other ([TaxProf Blog](https://taxprofblog.aals.org/2026/06/06/salinas-et-al-law-professors-prefer-ai-over-peer-answers/)). When the base model already knows the domain, grounding buys **verifiability more than raw quality**. Critics also note that answer preference is not learning impact ([David Kemp](https://davidkemp.ai/blog/answer-quality-is-not-learning-impact)).

Citation precision is strong on clean text and collapses on everything else. The one measured figure is 95% correct passage location. Users also report clicks that land at the top of a source instead of on the cited sentence, and there is no bibliography export or Zotero integration ([Citenook](https://citenook.com/blog/notebooklm-limitations/)). Scanned PDFs without a text layer need OCR before upload. Complex or merged-cell tables are flattened into prose. Charts, diagrams and handwriting inside PDFs are reportedly not analysed. LaTeX-rendered math often fails to extract ([Citenook](https://citenook.com/blog/notebooklm-limitations/); [notebooktoolkit](https://notebooktoolkit.com/blog/notebooklm-for-pdfs)). The common workaround is to convert PDFs to clean Markdown before uploading ([markdownconverters](https://markdownconverters.com/guides/upload-pdf-to-notebooklm)). That users have to do this at all is a market signal.

These are blog-level claims without controlled tests, and some may predate Gemini 3's multimodal abilities. Treat them as likely but unproven for late 2026. Vendor claims of "98% citation accuracy" have no traceable methodology and should be ignored ([elephas](https://elephas.app/blog/notebooklm-vs-chatgpt)).

Abstention is a design intent with no measurement behind it. NotebookLM is built to say when the sources don't cover a question, and Google's FAQ is quoted as excluding outside knowledge "by default" ([prompt-architects](https://prompt-architects.com/blog/182-notebooklm-prompt-templates-for-source-based-research)). Nobody has published a correct-abstention rate or a false-refusal rate. A hands-on test with a 90-page dissertation found it stayed grounded under adversarial prompts. The same test found that vague questions led to omitted details rather than refusals, and bad OCR undermined grounding ([daily.dev](https://daily.dev/posts/i-spent-an-afternoon-trying-to-make-notebooklm-hallucinate-and-i-was-genuinely-impressed-dyegkf0zy)).

Latency data is thin. No source gives time-to-first-token or full-answer times for chat, and the absence of complaints suggests chat feels fast. Studio is slow. Google says an Audio Overview "may take a couple of minutes" ([Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16212820?hl=en)). One review measured about **4 minutes for a 16-minute episode** from five sources ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/notebooklms-audio-overview-is-good-but-it-could-be-great/)), and another user reports more than 10 minutes on large notebooks ([Superlore](https://superlore.ai/blog/notebooklm-audio-overview-generation-time-2026)). Long YouTube lectures, very large PDFs and long audio files are slow to process or fail outright ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/notebooklm-limits/)).

## Hybrid long context and retrieval sits under a text-only core

Google has never documented NotebookLM's internals. The most defensible reconstruction combines staff statements, published limits and observed behaviour. Confidence varies by feature, as the table below shows.

The core is almost certainly **hybrid**. In October 2024 product lead Raiza Martin described the original system as "basically RAG" on a small context window. She praised Gemini 1.5's long-context ability, and also said that 50 sources of 500,000 words each could not all be jammed into the context window ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/notebooklm)). Co-founder Steven Johnson frames the design as "source grounding": filling the model's context window with the user's own documents ([Adjacent Possible](https://adjacentpossible.substack.com/p/introducing-notebooklm)).

The arithmetic forces the hybrid. A free notebook can hold 50 sources × 500,000 words, which is roughly 33M tokens and 30x the 1M-token window that chat has used since October 2025 ([blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/notebooklm-custom-personas-engine-upgrade/)). The most likely runtime policy has three steps. First, the source checkboxes act as a hard filter. Second, if the selected sources fit the window, they are sent whole, probably from a context cache. Third, if they don't fit, the system retrieves passages and fills the budget with them.

The October claim of a "50% improvement in satisfaction" on answers drawing on many sources fits a move toward putting more raw text in context. Nothing in the product tells the user which path a given answer took.

| Feature | Most likely mechanism | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chat | Selected sources sent whole when they fit ~1M tokens, retrieval when they don't; history budgeted separately ("6x memory") | Staff quote, limits arithmetic | Medium-high on hybrid; low on switching logic |
| Ingestion | Async job per source: fetch → extract to text with page, offset or time anchors → chunk → index → source-guide summary | 500k-*word* cap implies text normalisation; processing delay scales with size | Medium |
| Citations | Passage IDs with stored character offsets, rendered over extracted text; probably one ID scheme for both full-context and retrieval paths | Highlighting in the parsed-text viewer; "exact passages" claim from [Steven Johnson](https://x.com/stevenbjohnson/status/1740041487579271492) | Medium |
| Audio Overview | Multi-pass Gemini script (plan, draft, critique) with speaker tags and deliberate disfluencies → DeepMind dialogue TTS | Gemini 1.5 Pro script ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/29/notebooklm-audio-overview/)); DeepMind codec at ~600 bps ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/pushing-the-frontiers-of-audio-generation/)); Gemini 2.5 native audio cites NotebookLM ([blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-2-5-native-audio/)) | High on script → TTS; medium on passes |

| Video, slides, infographics | LLM-planned JSON storyboard → image model (Nano Banana / Nano Banana Pro) → Veo 3 animation for Cinematic → server-side compositing | [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/ai-image-video/google-notebooklm-can-now-turn-your-notes-into-ai-videos-visual-learners-will-love-this); [Geeky Gadgets](https://www.geeky-gadgets.com/gemini3-notebooklm-update/); image-only PPTX | High on models; medium on ordering |

The latency profile supports this picture. The reported DeepMind dialogue model renders two minutes of speech in under three seconds on one TPU, more than 40x real time ([AI Singapore mirror](https://learn.aisingapore.org/2024/10/pushing-the-frontiers-of-audio-generation/)). Most of a multi-minute Audio Overview wait must therefore go to queueing and sequential long-context LLM passes, not speech synthesis.

The 500,000-word limit, transcript-only YouTube and the text viewer all point the same way. Everything becomes text before indexing. Images and figures, at best, become captions. That makes citations and retrieval simple. It is also the root cause of every multimodal failure in the previous section.

## Learnin should copy the loop, beat the pixels, and expose the machinery

Learnin's design already rejects NotebookLM's main weakness. It routes scanned, table-heavy, figure-heavy and math-heavy PDF pages to Haiku 5.5 vision transcription. It falls back to yt-dlp plus speech recognition when a YouTube video has no captions. It extracts keyframes from video, and its Locator type carries page and bbox, timestamp, and text-fragment anchors (ARCHITECTURE.md §3.5, §4.2). The question is less whether Learnin can compete than which NotebookLM patterns it lacks and where it should differ on purpose.

### Copy five patterns that survived two years of feature growth

**First, adopt the three-zone model, with outputs as a first-class zone.** Learnin's web layout is "sources | chat | source viewer" (§10.2). NotebookLM shows that outputs need a home that can absorb new types as tiles without a redesign. The cheapest fit treats the right pane as a tabbed surface: the source viewer while a citation is open, and a Study panel for flashcards, quizzes, guides and later audio. That keeps the citation viewer Learnin needs while reserving space for the Phase 5 study tools. On Expo, the same zones become bottom tabs.

**Second, copy the source guide as an ingestion step.** Add a `summarize` step to the IngestSource workflow after `chunk`. Haiku 5.5 already holds the section text in a cached prefix for contextualisation (§4.3), so the step can produce a per-source summary plus five to eight key-topic chips. Each chip posts a scoped question into chat with `focus.sourceId` set. The same summaries can serve as a routing layer for notebooks that outgrow retrieval, the way NotebookLM likely uses them.

**Third, copy the notebook-level configuration that governs both chat and outputs.** Learnin already stores goals and level per notebook and a learner profile in the cached system prompt (§5.3, §5.5). Add NotebookLM's two knobs explicitly. A **goal mode** (Default / Tutor / Custom) maps to prompt variants under `packages/ai/prompts`. A **length** setting sits beside the Quick / Auto / Deep toggle, because NotebookLM's experts found its default answers the least succinct of six systems. Tutor mode should behave like Learning Guide and ask a check-for-understanding question back. That is the single most learning-specific behaviour in the product.

**Fourth, copy the handoff from outputs back into chat.** Every flashcard, quiz item and concept-map node should carry chunk IDs and open chat with a pre-filled "explain this" prompt and focus set to the cited chunk. One constraint makes this harder than it looks. Claude's native citations are incompatible with structured outputs (§5.2), so generated cards cannot get citations for free. The schema should require `chunk_ids[]` plus a short verbatim `quote` per item. A post-generation validator then rejects any item whose quote does not appear in the hydrated chunk text. That gives deterministic grounding for study material, stricter than anything NotebookLM is documented to do.

**Fifth, copy the review-then-import discovery flow, split into two speeds.** Learnin's Discover workflow already requires approval (§4.5). NotebookLM shows the value of two modes. **Quick** is a synchronous Haiku call with `web_search` that returns about ten annotated suggestions within the request. **Deep** is a long-running Workflow agent loop that writes a cited report, and the report itself becomes a `text` source with `added_via='discovery'`. Keep NotebookLM's bulk "remove failed sources" affordance. Learnin's `failed` and `partial` statuses already support it.

### Improve where Learnin's design already outruns NotebookLM, and close the gaps

**Citations should land on the page, not just the text, but the plan has a seam.** NotebookLM gets precise highlighting by giving up the original render. Learnin wants both: `react-pdf` with a bbox highlight on the original page. That works for text-layer pages if extraction keeps per-span coordinates, so that a Claude `cited_text` range maps back to a bbox. Vision-transcribed pages (scans, tables, math) produce Markdown with no coordinates. For those, the honest fallback is page-level highlighting in the original plus an exact-span highlight in a side "transcribed text" view.

Build that **dual view** (original | extracted) from the start rather than retrofitting it. It also gives users a way to audit transcription errors, which NotebookLM cannot show. For video and audio, the `{startMs, endMs}` locator that seeks the player is a clear win over NotebookLM's transcript-only citations. The eval set should include timestamp-precision checks, because that is exactly where NotebookLM's citations are reported to drift.

**Numerical comparison is a measured NotebookLM failure and an easy win.** When the router's intent is `derive` or `compare` and the retrieved chunks contain numbers or thresholds, give the model a sandboxed calculator or code tool. Store the computation in the message trace. NotebookLM only added code execution in July 2026, behind Ultra. Learnin can make it the default for quantitative questions, because its users are studying subjects like linear algebra.

**Make the full-context versus retrieval decision explicit and visible.** Learnin's design always retrieves (§5.4). NotebookLM's 2025 gains came from putting more raw text in context, and many of Learnin's real queries will scope to one or two sources: "explain lecture 3", or a `focus` on one PDF. Add a router branch. If the selected sources fit within a token budget, send them whole as cached `document` blocks; otherwise run the hybrid Qdrant pipeline.

Price it first. At Sonnet 5.5's $2/M input list price, an uncached 150k-token context costs about $0.30, ten times the $0.03 blended target (§1.2, §8.4). So the branch should require a warm prompt cache or a smaller budget, and the UserBudget Durable Object should account for it. Then do what NotebookLM never does: show it. Emit `route` with `contextMode: "full" | "retrieved"` and display "Read 2 sources in full" or "Searched 214 passages, used 9" above the answer. Users can then tell a gap in their sources from a gap in retrieval.

**Close the organisation gap NotebookLM left open for two years.** Learnin's model is one notebook per subject. Add tags or collections on notebooks and source-level pinning (`priority` already exists) from Phase 0 rather than later. It costs little and is the most complained-about missing feature. Learnin's `conversations` table already allows several named threads per notebook, where NotebookLM has one continuing thread. Keep that.

**Make mobile the best "listen and capture" client, then go further.** NotebookLM's app proves that share-sheet capture and audio playback are the mobile core. It is also criticised for losing the playback position and feeling thin. Learnin's planned share extension, direct-to-R2 uploads and push notification on ingest completion (§10.3) already beat it. When audio outputs arrive, persist the playback position server-side, allow offline downloads, and send a push when any long Studio job finishes, since NotebookLM sends nothing.

### Do differently: four deliberate departures

**Label every knowledge tier and measure abstention.** NotebookLM is source-only "by default" and still leaks unsupported interpretation into 13% of responses. Learnin already plans a labelled web fallback. Extend the same labelling to the model's own background knowledge. A student often needs the prerequisite the textbook skips. Render it as a visibly separate "Background (not from your sources)" block rather than banning it or blending it in.

Then measure what NotebookLM never has. The golden set already includes 10–15% unanswerable questions (§7). Report correct-abstention and false-refusal rates as headline metrics.

**Evaluate against NotebookLM's error taxonomy, at response level.** Learnin's groundedness target is at least 95% of claims supported (§1.2). NotebookLM's 13% is the share of *responses* with any unsupported claim. A ten-claim answer at 95% claim-level support can still easily contain one bad claim, so the two numbers don't compare. Track both. Add "interpretive overconfidence" and "attribution loss" as explicit labels for the Opus judge, and add a passage-location metric to compare against the 95% bar. A benchmark that checks only invented entities and numbers will flatter the system, as the newsroom study shows.

**Expose budgets instead of hiding them.** Google moved to an opaque compute budget that weighs "prompt complexity, chat length, number of sources, and the features you use". Learnin's UserBudget Durable Object already holds an exact dollar counter (§8.6). Show it. Display a cost estimate before an expensive generation such as a deep answer, a Deep Research run or an audio overview, and a running daily total. For a personal app paid per use, transparency is both cheaper to build and more honest than Google's design.

**Make study artifacts editable and scheduled, not just generated.** NotebookLM's slides export as images and its quizzes cannot be downloaded. The notes found no sign of spaced-repetition scheduling. Generate slides and study guides as Markdown or HTML that the user can edit. Schedule flashcards with `ts-fsrs` as Phase 5 already plans. Feed quiz misses into the derived notebook learning state (§5.5), so the tutor knows what the learner keeps getting wrong. Given the risk-of-bias result, keep any grading of free-text answers rubric-based and deterministic where possible, and show the rubric.

One structural idea is worth stealing outright. Google is turning notebooks into context that the Gemini app can use. A thin, read-only MCP endpoint over Learnin's retrieval API (search a notebook, fetch a cited passage) would let Claude's own apps use the same grounded corpus. It would add no side-effecting tools to the answer path.

## Conclusion

Retrieval is not what is hard to replicate in NotebookLM. Staff call its core "basically RAG", and its documented failures sit in ingestion and interpretation, not in search. What is hard to replicate is the **output factory**: about ten artifact types on one grounded corpus, rendered by Google's own speech, image and video models, and now distributed through the Gemini app to tens of millions of users. A one-person project cannot match that breadth, and Learnin shouldn't try before Phase 5. It can win depth for one learner. That means citations that land on the right page region or video second, ingestion that treats figures, tables and math as content, quantitative answers that are computed rather than guessed, and study loops that schedule and remember. Those are the places where NotebookLM's text-first architecture costs it the most.

The evidence also changes what "better" should mean. The law study suggests grounding buys verifiability more than raw answer quality, and its critics point out that nobody has measured learning outcomes. Google's switch to compute-metered limits confirms that generation, not storage, is where the money goes. That matches Learnin's own cost model, where audio and deep answers will dominate spending. Learnin's edge is therefore the instruments its design already includes: per-message traces, an evals gate and a dollar-exact budget. Pointed at the metrics NotebookLM has never published, such as response-level unsupported-claim rate, passage and timestamp precision, abstention accuracy and eventually retention, they let Learnin claim measurable superiority on the things a learner actually cares about.
